## The target is ten thousand players in one dimension

The goal is ten thousand players in one dimension with the unmodified vanilla client.[^181][^351] It is a design target, not a measured capacity of the current server.

The replication design budgets roughly 200 to 250 bytes per second before compression for each moving player a client sees. That estimate motivates spreading players across one dimension and bounding how many each client sees; it does not establish which subsystem limits the current server.

## Four parts, each in a small crate

The design rests on four ideas, and each is a small crate in the `mcrs_engine_*` family.

| Part | Crate | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Cell | `mcrs_engine_cell` | Owns a box of hot space. Only the owner changes the chunks in its box. |
| Link | `mcrs_engine_link` | Connects two parts. Messages go in and messages come out, and no part reads the state of another part. |
| Muxer | `mcrs_engine_muxer` | The gateway for clients. It owns the connections, decides what each client gets, and encodes a change once for all viewers of a tile. |
| Data layer | `mcrs_engine_store` | Holds the versioned chunks of cold space. |

A cell also holds a halo, which is a copy of the columns of its neighbours within two columns. With the halo a cell never changes a column it does not own. Where the far end of a link runs is called its placement, and the placement is `Inline` or `Thread`.

Do not confuse a cell with a shard. A cell is a unit of placement, a shard is the unit of causality described in [the sharding note](https://github.com/andreypfau/mcrs/blob/main/docs/minecraft-bevy-sharding-architecture.md), and one cell holds many shards.

Work inside a cell can already run in parallel, as [How a tick runs in parallel](/docs/en/concepts/parallel-tick/) shows. Cells are units of balance and placement. Moving a cell between processes or machines remains part of the design.

## What exists today

- A world has one cell, or a static division into several. The cells of all worlds tick in lockstep, either inline or each on a thread. The fields `worlds`, `cells` and `muxer` of `ServerCorePlugin` choose this, in `crates/mcrs_minecraft_engine_server/src/server_core_plugin.rs`.
- A muxer stage runs on a link from the host. The host talks to each dimension over a link, with one input and one output per tick.
- The data layer serves cold columns, with generation and light stages on a thread. A world with a save keeps its cold columns in the Anvil region files of each dimension, and a world without one keeps them in memory.
- The muxer sends player moves in four tiers, from every 2 ticks for the nearest to every 20 ticks for the farthest. It picks a tier by distance and moves a viewer to slower tiers when the viewer goes over its player byte budget. It encodes a tile once for each tier, and every viewer of that tier gets the same bytes. Mobs use their type's tracking range and a separate update path; they count toward neither the player cap nor that budget.
- A player moves to another cell at the end of the tick in which it stands in a column of that cell. There is no hysteresis yet, and no entity other than a player crosses.

Inside a cell, mobs are Bevy entities. Their publisher derives tile crossings from their positions; crossing a tile does not transfer a mob to another Bevy world. Players are Bevy entities too, and their separate publisher reads components to emit tile events. A tile projection is not the owner of a player's state.

## What is still design

- Split and merge of cells by cost. The design is a k-d division on X and Z, with moves at a tick boundary and a hysteresis of about 8 blocks.
- A cell in another process, together with an edge as its own process that holds every connection. This part is split into separate tasks: shards that run in parallel, and the steps of distribution from a link between processes to a coordinator.[^181][^160][^162][^167]

## The simplest server stays simple

All of this is optional. The design asks that a user can drop cells, the muxer and the data layer and write a server on one thread, with the game logic depending only on kernels and a world-view trait. [Layers and crates](/docs/en/concepts/layers/) explains what a kernel is.

Between that and the full layout there is a middle mode: the full engine with one cell per world and every link inline. It is deterministic, and it is the mode of the tests.
